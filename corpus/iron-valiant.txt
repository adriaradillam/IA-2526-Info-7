Iron Valiant stands out as a top-tier offensive Pokemon in OU, able to utilize its Speed; high attacking stats; and a wide movepool of setup, coverage, and utility to devastate any Pokemon that might attempt to check it. Its various sets, such as Swords Dance, Calm Mind, mixed, and Choice Specs, have very different counterplay from each other, and thus a well-played Iron Valiant can take out Pokemon such as

Zapdos

and

Galarian Slowking

that might want to check its Swords Dance and Calm Mind respectively. With Booster Energy, Iron Valiant becomes an incredible late-game cleaner and revenge killer, able to outspeed and subsequently threaten Pokemon such as

Zamazenta

, Dragon Dance

Dragonite

Darkrai

Hatterene

,

Gliscor

Iron Valiant can furthermore dip into its utility movepool, with options such as Encore, Destiny Bond, and Trick either to give it more setup turns, allow it to surprise typical counterplay even further, or emergency check opposing offensive threats. However, Pokemon such as Hatterene,

Moltres

and

can check the vast majority of its sets, while Pokemon such as

and Galarian Slowking are able to reliably take on specific sets such as Swords Dance and Calm Mind, respectively, once they have been identified. This above issue is made worse, as Iron Valiant also struggles to fit all the coverage options it wants into one set, especially if it decides to utilize both its setup and utility options. Furthermore, its low bulk impedes its sweeping potential, as priority users such as Dragonite,

Rillaboom

can revenge kill it once it has been weakened. This low bulk also means that Iron Valiant can struggle to find safe setup opportunities, as while it can theoretically force out Pokemon such as Zamazenta and

Draft Order

: Round 1 onwards

Price Range

: 19-20 points

Overview

: Iron Valiant is one of the most versatile offensive Pokemon in the format. With its great Fairy and Fighting typing, its strong mixed offensive stats, one of the deepest movepools in the game including offensive coverage and utility moves, and a great Speed tier that can be further boosted by Booster Energy, Iron Valiant can sometimes seem like an unstoppable force. It's topped off by having access to a very powerful Vacuum Wave, which on its own is an invaluable trait for a metagame filled with fast Dark-types. As a result, Iron Valiant is one of the premier options for first overall pick in SV draft because virtually everything else on the board pairs well with it.

Common Roles

Wallbreaker

: Iron Valiant is most commonly used as a wallbreaker with a damage boosting item such as Life Orb or Expert Belt. Its strong STAB moves paired with a Life Orb are very difficult to switch into on their own, but factoring in all its coverage options makes finding a counter basically impossible. These sets will almost always carry Vacuum Wave to help protect itself from being revenge killed by the many fast Dark-types in the metagame.

Speed Booster Energy Cleaner / Revenge killer

: With Speed Booster Energy, Iron Valiant outspeeds the entire unboosted metagame. This type of set aims to save Iron Valiant for late-game scenarios when the opponent's team is weakened. These sets make use of Iron Valiant's superb coverage to finish off the enemy team, and the Speed boost prevents Iron Valiant from being revenge killed. It often also slots utility moves like Encore, Trick, and Destiny Bond to remain dangerous to foes it cannot immediately KO or uses them to disrupt a foe as an emergency button in a pinch. These sets can even still make use of Vacuum Wave to hit opposing Choice Scarf users.

Setup Wincon

: These sets will also often run Speed Booster Energy but sacrifice one or two coverage moves for Calm Mind or Swords Dance and an optional utility move like Encore or Taunt. The tradeoff of less coverage for setup potential allows Iron Valiant to sweep teams without needing them as weakened. However, this requires finding an opportunity to set up, which is difficult due to Iron Valiant's subpar defensive profile. It's maybe even harder to justify setting up over just attacking with Iron Valiant's amazing raw power.

Niche Moves

Reflect and Light Screen

: Reflect and Light Screen can be run to have Iron Valiant take on a more supportive role on the off chance it has a really bad matchup against the opponent's team.

Hex

: Hex can be combined with Toxic Spikes or other status-spreading support to give Iron Valiant a strong Ghost-type coverage option. This is notably useful to break through bulky Psychic-type Pokemon that may carry a Colbur Berry, which can be decent defensive answers to Iron Valiant.

Hypnosis

: When legal, Hypnosis can be useful in matchups when the opposing team has two reasonable Iron Valiant checks that require different coverage moves and Iron Valiant can't beat either one-on-one. Hypnosis gives Iron Valiant an option to roll the dice on getting up to three free turns to transform losing matchups into winning ones. It can also be combined with Hex to break through troublesome bulky Psychic-type Pokemon such as

Galarian Slowking

.

Disable

: Disable is mostly only used as a surprise option to catch foes off guard, as it's less consistently useful than utility moves like Encore and Destiny Bond. It still has a great upside with potentially giving Iron Valiant free turns to do whatever it wants, but it can be difficult to justify.

Common Items

Life Orb / Expert Belt

: These damage-boosting items are used on mixed wallbreaker Iron Valiant sets, offering a boost to both of its offensive stats and not locking it into any single move. Life Orb offers more damage at the cost of longevity, so the better item for this role will depend on the matchup.

Booster Energy

: Most Iron Valiant wincon sets will run Speed Booster Energy to outspeed nearly the entire unboosted metagame, but Special Attack Booster Energy or Attack Booster Energy is also possible for wallbreaker sets.

Choice Specs / Choice Band

: On wallbreaker sets, Iron Valiant may just want to maximize its offense. Choice Specs sets are more common than their physical counterpart due to Iron Valiant's Vacuum Wave access, although both Choice items are plenty viable.

Niche Items

Choice Scarf

: It is mostly outclassed by Speed Booster Energy, but Iron Valiant can run Choice Scarf for more consistent speed control throughout the entirety of a game if needed. It also works well with Trick to cripple walls.

Heavy-Duty Boots

: If you expect your opponent to bring a team that will stack entry hazards, Heavy-Duty Boots is always a nice option to limit their effectiveness.

Air Balloon

: Iron Valiant can combine its natural Dragon immunity with Air Balloon's temporary Ground immunity to become immune to

Garchomp

's STAB moves, allowing it to safely switch in on Garchomp and threaten it out with a Fairy-type attack.

Focus Sash

: Iron Valiant is frail, so if it can enter the field at full HP, a Focus Sash could be a useful surprise option to guarantee Iron Valiant survives for more than one turn. It's most commonly seen on lead sets that look to reliably KO one foe immediately and then maybe try to trade for another with Destiny Bond, but it can still be used on non-lead sets when paired with entry hazard removal.

Tera

Iron Valiant is widely Tera-banned in Draft, so it is usually ineligible to be a Tera Captain. If you have the chance to let Iron Valiant Terastallize, it should definitely be a Tera Captain, as its already amazing offenses become extremely overwhelming. Virtually every Tera type can be helpful for Iron Valiant in some way thanks to its broad movepool. It can use Tera Fighting or Fairy to boost its already strong STAB moves, any Tera type that boosts chosen coverage moves like Tera Water to power up Liquidation, or a defensive Tera type like Steel to resist common forms of priority, making it harder to revenge kill.

Draft Strategy

Iron Valiant is a highly sought-after Pokemon, so it will most often be the first overall pick in a draft. Since it goes so early, it's important for Iron Valiant drafts to remain flexible, since many Pokemon will be taken before the draft returns to their pick. Luckily, Iron Valiant is incredibly flexible itself, so this isn't that hard to do.

Strong Alternate Wincon

: Iron Valiant's most consistent sets are its wallbreaking ones, so Iron Valiant drafts should take a more reliable setup threat to take advantage of the holes Iron Valiant rips through the opponent's team. Some options include

Dragonite

,

Kyurem

,

Ogerpon-W

, and

Iron Crown

.

Reliable Entry Hazards

: The threat of Iron Valiant's many coverage moves can force foes to switch, and stacking entry hazards will punish this heavily. It's already hard to switch into Iron Valiant late-game with a weakened team, and hazards make it even more painful.

Secondary Dragon-type Answer

: Iron Valiant is sometimes considered a fake Fairy-type; it's not bulky, and it has to be mindful of when to come onto the field when it's running Booster Energy. As a result, Dragon-types may not respect a team whose only Dragon-type answer is Iron Valiant, so a good Steel-type or a secondary Fairy-type, such as

Gholdengo

or

Tinkaton

, is appreciated.

Secondary Speed Control

: While Iron Valiant can be used as speed control with Speed Booster Energy, it often prefers a damage-boosting item or doesn't want to consume its Booster Energy before it's ready to sweep. Therefore, Iron Valiant should be paired with another source of speed control like strong priority users or a different fast Pokemon that can viably run Choice Scarf. Some common examples are

Greninja

,

Weavile

, and

Cinderace

Checks and Counters

Poison-types

: Iron Valiant's STAB combination is resisted by Poison-types, so bulky Poison-types like

Pecharunt

,

Muk

, and

Swalot

do a good job at handling Iron Valiant defensively. Iron Valiant can only threaten them with Psychic-type coverage, but without the STAB multiplier, most Poison-types will be able to survive a Psychic-type hit or two. Naturally, Iron Valiant benefits from being paired with strong Ground- or Psychic-type Pokemon to break through these Poison-types and discourage foes from using Tera Poison against it.

Resistance Berry Lures

: A common way of dealing with Iron Valiant's immense coverage is accepting Iron Valiant will hit its foes super effectively and using a Resistance Berry to survive a super effective hit. These are generally most effective on bulky Pokemon with an option to threaten Iron Valiant after surviving its attack, such as Galarian Slowking using Colbur Berry to survive Knock Off and OHKO it with Sludge Bomb.

Faster Choice Scarf Users / Priority

: To cover a Speed-boosted Iron Valiant, teams with a Pokemon with a better Speed tier than Iron Valiant can run a Choice Scarf to try to revenge kill it. Cleaner Iron Valiant sets will often try to save their Booster Energy until they're able to identify if a Choice Scarf user faster than Iron Valiant or a threatening priority move like Bullet Punch is on the opponent's team, and if one exists, they wait until it is no longer threatening for Iron Valiant to come onto the field. Iron Valiant's common use of Life Orb and vulnerability to entry hazards make it difficult to consistently stay out of strong priority moves' range.

To utilize Encore + Disable effectively, you usually have to use Protect on the first turn to scout the foe's first move. If you are certain it is a Choice-locked Pokemon, you can just use Disable on the second turn and force it to use Struggle, and if it utilizes a status move, you are free to use Encore on its move and attack with your main STAB move; otherwise, it is recommended to use Encore on the second turn to take a hit, then Protect on the third turn, then Disable on the fourth turn, and, once Encore is over, you use Encore again. This ensures 2 turns of Struggle from the foe, at which point Iron Valiant should be able to KO it. On more passive Pokemon that have moves that do little damage like Registeel, it sometimes can be better to opt out of using Protect and just use your main attack.
